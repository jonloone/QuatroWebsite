## The canonical one-liner

> **Quatro is the industrial AI integrator for the Americas. We integrate the intelligence stack your operators run their critical infrastructure on. Hardware, models, agents, and workflows, built with the operators who run them every shift.**

This is the gold standard. Every positioning surface should flow from this. If a piece of copy can't be derived from this one-liner, it's off-position.

---

## What each part does

**"Quatro is the industrial AI integrator for the Americas"**
The role + geography claim. "Industrial AI integrator" names the category — Quatro integrates the AI stack for industrial operators, not IT systems for general enterprises. "For the Americas" anchors the geographic scope (North America + Latin America) and signals where Quatro plays distinctively versus US-only competitors. The lowercase italic-emphasis hero treatment (per `QUATRO-PALETTE-V2-PROPOSAL.md`) reads as `industrial AI integrator *for the americas*` — modeled on Ark Robotics' `infrastructure layer *for mass robotics*`. Defense/B2B confidence move. No proper-case marketing-speak.

**"We integrate the intelligence stack"**
The noun-as-deliverable. The intelligence stack is the thing Quatro builds and the buyer gets. It's a stack (multiple layers) and it's intelligence (data + models + agents + workflows). Not a platform you configure. Not a utility you connect to. A stack we integrate. The verb "integrate" claims the integrator role; the noun "intelligence stack" claims the deliverable.

**"your operators run their critical infrastructure on"**
The operator-as-hero StoryBrand line. The stack is for *your operators* (possessive, second-person). They *run* (active verb — they're in control). *Their critical infrastructure* (the operator's domain, what they're responsible for). The structure puts the operator in the subject position of the verb of consequence. Quatro built it; the operator runs the operation on it.

**"Hardware, models, agents, and workflows"**
The stack components. Four named pieces: physical sensors and edge hardware; reasoning models; autonomous and assistive agents; the operational workflows that connect them. This list signals breadth (we span hardware to workflow) and specificity (these are the actual layers, not "everything"). Critically: no DataMiner, no DataOS, no xOps — those are upstream architecture, not buyer vocabulary.

**"built with the operators who run them every shift"**
The lightsaber metaphor literalized. "Built with" not "built for" — operators are co-builders, not just users. "Every shift" names the cadence — this is infrastructure-grade reliability, not occasional-use software. Closes the loop: the operators who built the stack are the same people who run on it, every shift, in perpetuity.

## Variants and extensions

The hero pattern admits two variant H1s per `QUATRO-PALETTE-V2-PROPOSAL.md`:

- `industrial AI integrator *for the americas*` — role+geo lead. Most direct application of the Ark Robotics template.
- `the intelligence stack *built with operators*` — deliverable+co-builder lead. Closer to StoryBrand operator-as-hero.

Both work. Pick by surface intent: role+geo for top-of-funnel buyer education, deliverable+co-builder for operator-recognition surfaces.

The one-liner supports compression for tight contexts:

- **Twitter / outbound subject line:** "The industrial AI integrator for the Americas."
- **One-liner for a deck:** "We integrate the intelligence stack your operators run their critical infrastructure on."
- **Single-sentence company description:** "Quatro is the industrial AI integrator for the Americas, building the intelligence stack with the operators who run it every shift."

## Outcome word

**"Intelligent operations"** — the operator's win. What your team runs on the stack. Use in CTA closers, dual-CTA descriptions, mission language, hero subhead closers. StoryBrand-compliant (operator-as-hero — your team is operating intelligently, not Quatro delivering intelligence). Compatible with the May 15 canonical.

Use **"operational intelligence"** as the deliverable category in product/technical contexts where the buyer is searching for the existing category (SEO and analyst recognition). Both terms can coexist — they have different jobs.

## Voice rules (still active — apply to all positioning copy)

From `jonathan-voice` skill + `feedback_voice_rules.md`:

- **Banned vocabulary:** `empower`, `enable`, `power`/`powered`/`powering`, `unlock`, `delve`, `landscape` (as decoration), `tapestry`, `pivotal`, `underscore`, `testament`, `genuinely`, `honestly`, `straightforward`, `complexity`, `handle`.
- **No em-dash as connective tissue.** Em-dashes for parenthetical asides are OK. Em-dashes as the connector between two clauses where a period or colon would work are not.
- **No rhetorical tricolons in body copy.** "X. Y. Z. {payoff}" patterns banned. Three-step plan labels (Connect. Understand. Act.) are fine — they're labels, not rhetoric.
- **No "not X, Y" antitheses** unless the construction *is* the positioning differentiator (rare exception, must earn its keep).
- **Operator is the grammatical subject** of action verbs whenever possible. "Your operators run," "your team works from," "your dispatcher sees."

From `feedback_reckless_caps.md`: **No all-caps Reckless** for display type. The May 15 hero treatment is lowercase Roobert with italic emphasis on the qualifier phrase.

## Service naming pattern

From `QUATRO-SERVICE-NAMING.md`:

- **Two words maximum:** Grid Health, Pipeline Integrity, Fleet Health, Revenue Assurance.
- **No suffix words:** no "Intelligence," "Product," "Platform," "Module," "Solution," "System."
- **Outcome-named, not mechanism-named.**
- **Service framing:** buyers "connect" services on the stack, like subscribing to broadband.

## What this doc explicitly does NOT do

This is positioning vocabulary — what Quatro IS and what we build. It does not include:

- **Industry-specific pain copy** — that lives in industry .md files and uses the operator-pain headlines we landed in the industry headline audit.
- **Solution detail copy** — solution .md files use operator-outcome H1s ("Find the leak before pressure drops at the tap").
- **Use-case body copy** — those follow their own structure (pain narrative, capabilities, proof point).
- **Visual design specifications** — see `QUATRO-DESIGN-LANGUAGE-V2.md` and `QUATRO-PALETTE-V2-PROPOSAL.md`.

This doc is the *anchor*. Industry, solution, and use-case copy should be operator-pain-anchored (cold-read surfaces) with this canonical positioning as the shared category language. Don't repeat the one-liner on every page. Use it on positioning anchor surfaces (homepage, About, Partners, Technology) and let derived copy on industry/solution pages stay operator-first.

## Process: how to use this doc

When writing any Quatro positioning copy:

1. **Read the one-liner first.** Every claim you make should be derivable from it or compatible with it.
2. **Check the rejected vocabulary list.** If the term you're about to use is on it, stop. Use the canonical replacement.
3. **Check the voice rules.** If the construction violates jonathan-voice, fix it before shipping.
4. **For visual treatment**, see `QUATRO-DESIGN-LANGUAGE-V2.md` and `QUATRO-PALETTE-V2-PROPOSAL.md`. The lowercase italic-emphasis hero pattern is the canonical hero typography.
5. **When uncertain**, ask: "is this claim something the May 15 canonical would say?" If you can't answer yes, you're off-position.
